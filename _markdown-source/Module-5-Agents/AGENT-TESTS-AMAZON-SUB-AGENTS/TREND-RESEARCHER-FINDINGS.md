# Trend Researcher Findings: Pro-Callout Evidence Search

**Date:** 2025-11-27
**Agent:** Trend Researcher (1 of 8)
**Objective:** Find credible evidence supporting callouts, swing tags, and busy images on Amazon main images

---

## Executive Summary

After extensive searching, **no peer-reviewed academic evidence** was found supporting callouts, swing tags, or text overlays on Amazon MAIN images. The evidence that does exist for "infographic-style" images specifically relates to **secondary images**, not main images.

**Critical Finding:** Amazon explicitly PROHIBITS text, graphics, watermarks, and promotional badges on main images. The echo chamber may be conflating what works on secondary images with main image strategy.

---

## Key Findings

### 1. Amazon's Official Policy on Main Images

> "Amazon prohibits added text, graphics, watermarks, or promo badges on main images. The image should be a professional photo of the product itself, not an infographic or advertisement."

**Source:** [EcomClips - How to Improve Amazon CTR](https://ecomclips.com/blog/how-to-improve-your-amazon-ctr-with-main-image/)

**Main Image Requirements:**
- Pure white background (RGB 255, 255, 255)
- Professional photograph of actual product
- NO text, logos, borders, color blocks, watermarks
- NO graphics over the product or background
- Product must be outside packaging

**Sources:**
- [SellerMate - Amazon Image Requirements 2025](https://www.sellermate.ai/post/amazon-image-requirements-product-photos)
- [Jungle Scout - Amazon Image Requirements 2024](https://www.junglescout.com/resources/articles/amazon-image-requirements/)

---

### 2. Where Callouts/Badges ARE Appropriate

**Secondary Images (Slots 2-7):**
- Infographics with feature callouts
- Benefit-focused text overlays
- Comparison charts
- Instructional images
- Lifestyle context images

> "Text overlays are not allowed on the main product image. However, secondary images can include text overlays to highlight features, benefits, or instructions."

**Source:** [Soona - Text Overlay for Amazon](https://soona.co/blog/text-overlay-for-amazon-product-listings)

---

### 3. The "Badge" Workaround

Some sellers add badges to **product packaging itself** (printed on the physical box/label) which then appears in the main image. This is technically compliant but different from digital overlays.

Examples cited:
- "Made in USA" on physical packaging
- "30-day supply" printed on product label
- "All Natural" badges on actual product

**Source:** [My Amazon Guy - Main Image CTR](https://myamazonguy.com/amazon-ctr/)

---

### 4. A9 Algorithm & Image Preferences

> "High-quality product pictures help improve your SEO ranking on Amazon. The A9 algorithm considers product images when ranking products."

**Algorithm factors:**
- Image resolution (min 1000px, optimal 2000x2000px)
- Zoom capability
- File name keywords
- Alt text optimization
- User engagement metrics (CTR, time on page)

**No evidence found** that busy/cluttered images improve A9 rankings. The opposite is suggested:

> "Image quality has a direct impact on conversion rates and search rankings. Amazon's algorithm favors listings with better engagement metrics."

**Source:** [KwickMetrics - A9 Algorithm Guide 2024](https://www.kwickmetrics.com/blog/2024-guide-to-amazon-a9-algorithm)

---

### 5. Successful Amazon Brand Image Strategies

**Anker (Most successful Amazon-native brand):**
> "Anker keeps its branding clean and professional, with a minimalistic design and a focus on reliability and tech innovation."

> "They advertise their products simply—no grand claims—but when customers receive them, they're often impressed."

**Results:** $954 million revenue (2019), 46 million units sold, consistent #1 BSR in power banks

**Native (Natural Deodorant):**
> "Every bit of their Amazon presence screams 'clean, simple, and effective.'"

> "Uses earthy, pastel colors that scream 'clean and natural' in every image."

**Source:** [eStoreFactory - What Amazon Sellers Can Learn from Anker](https://www.estorefactory.com/blog/what-can-amazon-sellers-learn-from-brands-like-apple-anker/)

---

### 6. Testing Platform Guidance

**PickFu's Official Recommendation:**
> "To avoid overcluttering your main image or not meeting Amazon requirements, save detailed information for your secondary images. Include elements like product specifications, text callouts, and images showing the product in use in these secondary slots. This keeps your main image clean and focused."

**Source:** [PickFu - Amazon Main Image Optimization](https://www.pickfu.com/amazon-main-image-optimization)

---

### 7. Infographic Impact (Secondary Images Only)

Statistics often cited for "infographic success":
- "Visuals can increase conversion rates by up to 80%"
- "Infographics are 30 times more likely to be read than text"
- "65% of buyers are visual learners"

**Critical Context:** These statistics apply to **secondary images and A+ content**, not main images. The sources specifically discuss infographic slots 2-7.

**Sources:**
- [My Amazon Guy - Amazon Listing Infographics](https://myamazonguy.com/amazon-design/amazon-listing-infographics/)
- [Jungle Scout - Amazon Product Infographics](https://www.junglescout.com/resources/articles/amazon-product-infographic/)

---

## Conclusion

**No credible evidence exists** supporting callouts, swing tags, or busy compositions on Amazon MAIN images. The evidence that exists for infographic-style imagery specifically applies to:

1. Secondary image slots (2-7)
2. A+ Content modules
3. Brand Storefronts

The echo chamber appears to be:
1. Conflating secondary image best practices with main image strategy
2. Sharing TOS-violating main images that may be at risk of suppression
3. Cherry-picking test winners without showing the full dataset
4. Ignoring that successful brands (Anker, Native) use clean professional imagery
